# Hologram lab

Open `/lab/hologram` in development to tune content opacity, the minimum opacity in front of the skull, fade reach, frame glow, light on the skull and soft shadow strength. Frames remain fully opaque while the video fades uniformly across each card. The fade follows the projected card center and skull bounds with a depth check; it never cuts a skull-shaped hole into the image.

**Keep variant** stores a configuration in the session comparison rail. **Reset** restores the saved site configuration. **Save to site** writes `src/config/orbitSignal.json` and copies the JSON when clipboard access is available. Both the site and lab use those settings. The editor and save endpoint are unavailable in production.

The default orbit has 10 cards with 42% of each slot reserved for a gap. The lab exposes card count, gap, card size, radius, vertical offset and both tilt angles. Card size scales both dimensions together; video retains its aspect ratio and is fitted entirely inside the card. Saved settings apply on the main site as well as the lab.

Layout changes update the rendered cards, collision dimensions, local illumination and entrance origin together. Collision history resets when card dimensions change to avoid treating a slider edit as impact velocity.

The glow mesh extends past every edge and the halo fades to zero before that mesh ends. `src/lib/skullOrbitLighting.ts` adds approximate local illumination and proximity occlusion from nearby card surfaces and luminous edges to the glass shader. This follows the orbit rotation, gaps, entrance, fade and displaced skull fragments. It is a local shading approximation, not scene-wide screen-space AO or a shadow map.

At grazing angles the shader caps the antialiasing width and attenuates the halo, preventing a nearly edge-on card from producing an opaque vertical streak.

The **Card style** selector compares the classic hologram with **Cyberpunk HUD**. The HUD uses chamfered corners, red structural lines, cyan brackets, numbered project labels and a translucent dark surround. Video fits inside a proportional inset, so the housing never crops the project. **HUD detail intensity** controls the secondary markings. The style is saved with the other settings; local lighting on the skull follows its palette.

HUD labels are drawn once into a shared canvas texture atlas (`src/lib/projectOrbitHud.ts`), with a unique number per card. They follow the same curved surface and mirrored back face as the video. The atlas is disposed when the orbit unmounts.

**Glitch intensity** restores the original Featured Projects horizontal slice jitter and RGB separation across the complete card, including its HUD, frame and halo. Each card uses a different time offset; the effect runs during entrance and idle. The default intensity is 0.38; zero disables it, and reduced motion always disables it. Shifted rows use the padded curved surface, so they can extend outside the original frame.

**Glitch scope** switches between **Whole card** (the default) and **Image / video only**. Media-only keeps the frame, HUD and halo stable while jitter and RGB separation affect the image inside its media window. The scope is saved by **Save to site** and restored with kept variants.

After the entrance, drag a card to rotate the ring along its projected orbit direction. Tracking is immediate; release velocity comes from the last 90 ms of movement. A fast release carries momentum in either direction and decays exponentially toward idle. Holding still for 120 ms before release cancels the throw. Re-grabbing stops momentum immediately. Pointer cancellation, lost capture, leaving the hero, hiding the tab and window blur cancel the gesture. Touch keeps native vertical scrolling, and reduced motion allows direct dragging without autoplay or release inertia. The skull remains fixed.

The hero exit shares one scroll envelope (`heroAssemblyAt`). Between 2.5% and 65% of the hero transition the tilted cylinder unwraps into a horizontal ribbon with flat cards. Scrolling contributes 1.8 turns on top of the ongoing rotation; stopping scroll leaves idle or release momentum running. Card opacity stays unchanged until 68%, then fades by 88%. At the same time, the skull fragments move toward scattered spring targets and shrink individually during the final fade. The hero retains most of its scale instead of collapsing early. Scroll reversal reconstructs the assembly without replaying the intro; the details skull resets its simulation while hidden at the 90% stage boundary. Reduced motion retains the late fade but removes scroll spin, unwrapping and scattering. Collision and local lighting use the same curved-to-flat coordinates as the ribbon.

Entrance has two stages: 450 ms of sustained momentum followed by 900 ms of braking. The brake integrates a quintic smoothstep velocity curve, preserving velocity and acceleration at both joins. The ring travels 1.15 turns plus the idle drift, closing the loop before braking finishes. It starts at 10% of the skull entrance (120 ms into the default 1.2-second skull scale), then finishes settling after the scale animation. Opacity reaches 95% in approximately 214 ms; scroll-exit damping is unchanged. Idle runs at 0.11 rad/s (57.1 seconds per turn, approximately 9.5 seconds per slot with six cards). Its angular velocity is continuous at the handoff and independent of frame rate down to 10 FPS; frame gaps above 100 ms are skipped to prevent resume jumps. These values are tuned from the motion curves, not measured fragment-settling times; the fragments use a separate physical simulation.
